Try it R(3,3) = 6

Colour every edge without making a one-colour triangle.

Tap an edge to make it blue, tap again for red, once more to clear it. With 5 points it can be done. With 6 points it can't, however you try. That is what R(3,3) = 6 means.

My papers ask the same question with more colours and bigger groups. Colour every edge between 198 points with three colours and some 4 points are always joined in one colour: R(4,4,4) ≤ 198.

12 of 15 edges coloured 1 one-colour triangle

Points 1, 2 and 6 form an all-blue triangle. Recolour one of its edges and keep going.

Ramsey Numbers Research 2.0

Two preprints with new bounds for small Ramsey numbers, including R(4,4,4) ≤ 198 (previously 229), R(3,4,4) ≤ 73 (previously 77), R(4,21) ≥ 260 and R(4,24) ≥ 370. The main new tool is a flag-algebra certificate with local caps on colour neighbourhoods. Every computer-assisted claim ships with certificates re-checked in exact arithmetic by independent verifiers.

Ramsey Numbers Research

COMP3821 (Extended Algorithm Design and Analysis) research project on computational bounds for Ramsey numbers and properties of extremal Ramsey graphs. I disproved the Extension Conjecture and the Degree Difference Conjecture by counterexample search on McKay's graph database, and wrote a 3-colour Ramsey graph generator to find lower bounds.

Apr 2026computability theory / 2D→1D compiler / transition rules / tape automata

Turing Machine Simulator

Write transition rules in a plain-text format, load a tape, and step through the universal model of computation. Includes a 2D mode whose star feature is a 2D→1D compiler: any 2D Turing program is mechanically rewritten into an equivalent 1D program that lays each row out between separators and shuffles head markers around — a constructive proof that the extra dimension buys you no extra power.
